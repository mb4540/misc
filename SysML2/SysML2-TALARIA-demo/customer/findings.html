Plain reading: a ceramic capacitor's applied voltage must be at or below 75% of its rated voltage. At 48 V against a 50 V part, the allowed figure is 37.5 V, so this fails by 10.5 V.

Applied voltage of 48 V occurs only during a 200 ms hot-swap inrush window. Steady-state applied voltage is 5.1 V, well inside the Class B limit. Accelerated life testing on the same MPN shows no measurable degradation at 96% of rated for 106 cycles.

Alternative: Rev D substitutes a 100 V part in the same footprint, which clears the limit outright. Lead time 9 weeks.

The customer can see the actual rule
Because the rule is a formal expression rather than a factor plus interpreting code, it can be shown to the customer verbatim. "Here is what we checked" becomes a literal statement instead of a paraphrase of what the software did.
Today: the customer sees the numbers and the standard reference. Showing the rule itself would mean showing a TypeScript function, which is not a meaningful disclosure.
2
Provenance travels with the constraint
The clause reference is an attribute of the rule, so every verdict cites its source automatically rather than relying on the report generator to look it up.
Today: this one is largely in place. derating_rule carries documentId and sectionCode into document_section, and the ingestion pipeline maintains it through a human review gate.
3
Named placements, not a count
Each affected capacitor is a distinct part usage, so the customer sees which placements are involved and can act on a subset. Accepting an exception for six and replacing thirteen is expressible.
Today: the designator string is expanded at analysis time and the finding reports a group. Partial disposition has nowhere to be recorded.
4
Derived stresses, not asserted numbers
With ports and connections modelled, applied power is computed from the design rather than entered alongside it. The customer's obvious question — "how did you arrive at 0.78 W?" — has a traceable answer.
Today: there is no connectivity model. actualPower is a number with no modelled origin, and this is the largest genuine gap in the current schema.
5
Waivers as part of the delivered model
An approved exception is metadata on the part it excuses, so it survives export. The customer's own insurer or authority reads the same artefact and sees the same disclosure.
Today: the waiver table is well-modelled and complete, but it stays in the database. What leaves is a PDF describing it.

Is the 48 V a real risk or a paperwork problem?
RAPDA
Mostly paperwork, with a caveat. Steady-state is 5.1 V — comfortably inside the limit. The 48 V is a 200 ms inrush transient. EEE-INST-002 Table 3-2 does not distinguish transient from steady-state, so the constraint fails as written. The engineering argument for the exception is sound; the standard just has no clause for it.
James Wilson
What does accepting it cost me at the insurance review?
RAPDA
It appears in the evidence pack as an accepted exception with its justification and the accelerated life data attached. Insurers generally treat a documented transient exception with test backing far more favourably than an undocumented pass — but that is their judgement, not mine.
