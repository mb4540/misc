11. Nonconformance & Corrective Action

Containing, dispositioning, and root-causing nonconforming product — and closing the loop with corrective action and lessons learned

Student Introduction

Nonconformance and corrective action is where AS9100D gets very concrete: when something does not meet a specified requirement, this procedure defines exactly what happens next — stop, tag, contain, disposition, root-cause, correct, verify, and close. Any Jemba9 employee can identify a nonconformance, and every employee is expected to know the first three steps cold: stop work on the item if it's safe to do so, tag it with a red "HOLD — NONCONFORMING" tag, and notify the Quality Manager or designee immediately. That first response is what clause 8.7 calls containment, and it has to happen fast — within 4 hours for a safety-related nonconformance, same business day for everything else.

The Nonconformance Report (NCR, form QF-002) is the single record that carries a nonconforming item from discovery through disposition. For hardware, the same form doubles as a Nonconforming Material Report (NMR) — this matters at J9CM, where a PCB assembly with an out-of-tolerance dimension or a failed in-circuit test gets tagged and dispositioned exactly the same way as any other space hardware nonconformance. The Quality Manager decides the disposition — Use As Is, Rework, Repair, Scrap, or Return to Supplier — and any Use As Is or Repair disposition requires Chief Engineer sign-off, plus customer approval if the contract requires it. That extra layer of engineering review exists because "good enough for this application" is a much higher bar on a satellite than on commercial ground equipment; the same defect that would be waived without a second thought in a terrestrial product can be a mission-ending decision in orbit.

Disposition closes out the physical item, but it does not by itself fix the underlying cause. That is the job of the Corrective Action Request (CAR, form QF-003), which is triggered when a nonconformance recurs, affects safety or mission criticality, results in customer notification, comes out of an audit finding, or has a systemic root cause. A CAR requires genuine root cause analysis — 5-Why, Fishbone, Is/Is-Not, or Fault Tree Analysis — and the procedure is explicit that "human error" alone is never an acceptable root cause; the analysis has to go deep enough that a specific action can eliminate the cause, not just discipline the person. The CAPA Owner implements the corrective actions, and critically, the effectiveness check that verifies the fix actually worked is performed by the Quality Manager, not the CAPA Owner — independence in verification is what keeps a CAPA from being marked "closed" when the root cause is still live.

The default CAPA due date is 30 calendar days, and this ties directly to two of Jemba9's tracked Quality Objectives: QO-06 (CAPA Closure ≥ 90% within 30 days) and QO-07 (Nonconformance Rate trending down). Both are reviewed monthly by the Quality Manager and reported at Management Review, so how well this process runs shows up directly in the numbers leadership tracks. The final piece is the Lessons Learned Log (QF-016), which captures the insight from an NCR, audit finding, or program close-out and feeds it forward — a lesson from one program's supplier nonconformance or design failure gets reviewed at Management Review and can change how the next program is run, which is the practical meaning of clause 10.3 continual improvement.

Who needs this training: All employees need the basic "stop, tag, notify" awareness; Quality Manager, CAPA Owners, Chief Engineer, Manufacturing Manager, and Supply Chain Manager need the full disposition, root-cause, and CAPA workflow.

Syllabus & Training Goals

Class Syllabus

  1. Key definitions: nonconformance, NCR/NMR, containment, disposition, root cause analysis, corrective action, CAPA, effectiveness check
  2. Immediate response: stop, tag "HOLD — NONCONFORMING," notify Quality Manager, and containment timing (4 hours safety-related, same business day otherwise)
  3. Completing the NCR (QF-002): header fields, description of nonconformance, containment, and scope-of-impact assessment
  4. Disposition options and authority: Use As Is/Waiver, Rework, Repair, Scrap, Return to Supplier — and when Chief Engineer or customer sign-off is required
  5. When corrective action is required vs. disposition-only closure (recurrence, safety/mission impact, customer notification, audit finding, systemic cause)
  6. Root cause analysis methods (5-Why, Fishbone, Is/Is-Not, FTA) and why "human error" alone is never an acceptable root cause statement
  7. The Corrective Action Request (QF-003): problem statement, containment, RCA, corrective action plan, implementation evidence, effectiveness check, closure
  8. CAPA escalation timeline (30/45/60-day notifications) and independent effectiveness verification by the Quality Manager
  9. Lessons Learned Log (QF-016) and the link to Quality Objectives QO-06 (CAPA closure) and QO-07 (nonconformance rate)

Learning Objectives

By the end of this session, participants will be able to:

  • Correctly perform the immediate stop/tag/notify response when a nonconformance is found
  • Complete an NCR (QF-002) with a clear, factual description tied to the specific requirement not met
  • Identify which disposition applies and who must approve it
  • Determine whether a nonconformance requires a full CAPA (QF-003) or disposition-only closure
  • Write a root cause statement that is specific and verifiable, not a restatement of "human error"
  • Explain how CAPA closure and nonconformance rate performance connect to QO-06 and QO-07

Recommended audience:

All employees (basic awareness) Quality Manager CAPA Owners Chief Engineer Manufacturing Manager Supply Chain Manager J9CM production staff

Suggested periodicity: Basic awareness at new-hire onboarding with an annual refresher; full NCR/CAPA workflow training for CAPA-eligible roles annually, or immediately upon assignment as a CAPA Owner.
