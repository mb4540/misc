13. Management Review

Where the entire QMS converges for leadership decision-making, quarterly

Student Introduction

Every other topic in this training program feeds into this one. Management Review, required by AS9100D clause 9.3, is the quarterly meeting where Jemba9's top management — the CEO, chairing, together with the Quality Manager, Program Managers, Chief Engineer, Manufacturing Manager, and Supply Chain Manager — steps back from day-to-day operations and asks a single question: is the Quality Management System still suitable, adequate, and effective, and what do we need to change?

What makes this session different from every other topic is that it produces almost nothing new on its own. Instead, it pulls together data that other processes have already generated: open action items from the last review; changes in Jemba9's business context and interested-party needs (QP-POL-002, QP-POL-003); the Quality Objectives dashboard (QP-OBJ-001) covering on-time delivery, customer satisfaction, first pass yield, and more; NCR and CAPA performance and trends (QP-009); internal audit results and open findings (QP-010); supplier performance, ASL changes, and counterfeit-parts events (QP-005, QP-006); the status of open High and Critical risks in the Risk Register (QP-003); resource and training adequacy; and lessons learned and improvement opportunities surfaced from programs and audits. Management Review is the place all of that converges, quarter after quarter, so leadership makes decisions with the full picture rather than isolated fragments of it.

The output side matters just as much as the input side. AS9100D requires that Management Review produce documented decisions, not just discussion: decisions on improvement opportunities, resource needs (staffing, training, tooling, infrastructure), changes to the QMS itself, adjustments to Quality Objectives, and a formal reconfirmation (or revision) of the Quality Policy's continued suitability. Each decision is assigned an owner and a due date in the Management Review Minutes (QF-005), which the Quality Manager issues within five business days and the CEO approves before distribution via SharePoint. Overdue action items past 30 days escalate directly to the CEO — there is no quiet way for a Management Review commitment to be forgotten.

Because this is a leadership-level decision forum rather than an operational training session, the specific mechanics of it — agenda structure, KPI dashboard format, minutes template — are not something most employees need to memorize. What every employee does benefit from understanding is that the data they generate in their own corner of the QMS (an NCR they raised, an audit finding they closed, a supplier scorecard they compiled, a risk they flagged) is not filed away and forgotten; it surfaces here, quarterly, in front of the CEO, as part of how Jemba9 actually runs the business.

Who needs this training: CEO and the executive leadership team, and the Quality Manager who prepares, facilitates, and documents the review. This is not a training every employee takes.

Syllabus & Training Goals

Class Syllabus

  1. Purpose and scope of Management Review under clause 9.3; required and as-applicable attendees; quarterly minimum frequency plus triggers for an off-cycle review (major customer NCR, loss of certification scope, significant organizational change)
  2. Building the input package: the Quality Manager's five-business-day preparation cycle and the nine required input categories (§2.1–2.9 of QP-011)
  3. Status of prior actions: how open items from the last review are tracked and reported, and what "closed / in progress / overdue" means for accountability
  4. Reading the Quality Objectives dashboard (QP-OBJ-001, QO-01 through QO-10): on-time delivery, customer satisfaction, first pass yield, supplier OTD, audit completion, CAPA closure rate, NCR rate, incoming rejection rate, training completion, product safety coverage
  5. NCR/CAPA performance and internal audit results as inputs, and how they connect back to QP-009 and QP-010
  6. Supplier performance and risk register status as inputs, connecting back to QP-005 and QP-003
  7. Required outputs per §4: improvement opportunities, resource needs, QMS changes, Quality Objectives adjustments, and the Quality Policy suitability confirmation
  8. Completing and approving the Management Review Minutes (QF-005): five-business-day issuance, CEO approval, SharePoint filing, and next-review scheduling
  9. Action item governance: ownership, due dates, and the 30-day overdue escalation to the CEO

Learning Objectives

By the end of this session, participants will be able to:

  • List the nine required Management Review input categories and name the QMS process that supplies each one
  • Explain the difference between design verification-style QMS metrics (e.g., first pass yield) and the higher-level suitability/adequacy/effectiveness judgment Management Review makes
  • Describe the five required output categories and give an example decision for each
  • State the review frequency, who chairs it, and the escalation path for an overdue action item
  • Locate the Management Review Minutes (QF-005) template and identify where each required output is documented

Recommended audience:

CEO Executive leadership team Quality Manager

Suggested periodicity: Once at appointment to an executive leadership role or the Quality Manager position; brief refresher at the start of each calendar year to align on the coming year's review schedule and any QP-011 revisions.
