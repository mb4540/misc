12. Internal Audit

How Jemba9 verifies its own QMS is working — audit program, auditor qualification, and the link to CAPA and Management Review

Student Introduction

Internal audit is how Jemba9 checks its own work: clause 9.2 requires the company to periodically verify that the QMS both conforms to AS9100D and to Jemba9's own procedures, and that it is effectively implemented and maintained. It is not the same thing as the external certification audit run by the registrar, and it is not a customer quality audit — it is Jemba9's own internal check, run on a rolling 12-month cycle that is required to touch every process area in the QMS at least once, from Document Control and Risk Management through Supplier Management, Counterfeit Parts Prevention, NCR & CAPA, and Management Review itself.

The Quality Manager builds and owns the Annual Internal Audit Schedule, but audit frequency is not fixed — a process with recent findings, a higher inherent risk level, or a recent significant change gets audited more often than the annual minimum. This is risk-based thinking (see Topic 3) applied to the audit program itself: the areas most likely to have a problem get looked at hardest.

The independence requirement in clause 9.2.1 is a hard rule, not a suggestion: auditors must not audit their own work. In a company Jemba9's size, true separation is achieved through cross-functional auditing — Manufacturing audits Quality processes, Quality audits Engineering processes, and so on — and the Quality Manager may audit almost any process except NCR & CAPA, specifically to avoid auditing their own CAPA closures. Auditors also have to be qualified: trained on AS9100D requirements, familiar with the process being audited, and trained in audit technique, with all of it documented in the Training Record (QF-010).

Findings are classified as Major Nonconformance, Minor Nonconformance, or Observation/OFI, and every Major or Minor finding triggers a real NCR under QP-009 within 5 business days — this is the direct link between internal audit and Topic 11's nonconformance and corrective action process. A Major finding also triggers a follow-up audit within 90 days to verify the fix actually worked. None of this happens in isolation: the audit schedule, open findings, and CAPA status on audit-driven NCRs are standing agenda items at Management Review (QP-011, Topic 13), which is how audit results actually influence company decisions rather than sitting in a report nobody reads.

Who needs this training: This is a narrow-audience topic, not a company-wide one. Only personnel designated and trained as internal auditors — per the training matrix in QF-010 — need the full audit-conduct training below. Process owners who will be audited benefit from a short separate briefing on what to expect (not covered by this syllabus), but general staff do not need this session.

Syllabus & Training Goals

Class Syllabus

  1. Purpose and scope of internal audit (clause 9.2) and how it differs from certification audits and customer quality audits
  2. The rolling 12-month audit program, minimum coverage table, and factors that increase audit frequency (prior findings, risk level, process change, MR inputs)
  3. Building and approving the Annual Internal Audit Schedule and where it is posted (SharePoint)
  4. Auditor independence (9.2.1): the "must not audit your own work" rule and how Jemba9 achieves cross-functional independence, including the Quality Manager's NCR & CAPA exclusion
  5. Auditor qualification requirements and the QF-010 training record
  6. Audit preparation: reviewing procedures, prior findings, related NCRs/CAPAs, and building the audit checklist (2-week lead time)
  7. Conducting the audit: opening meeting, evidence-gathering methods (document review, record sampling, observation, interview), closing meeting
  8. Finding classification (Major / Minor / Observation-OFI) and the Internal Audit Report (QF-004) content and 5-business-day issuance deadline
  9. Corrective action on findings: NCR-raising deadline, default CAPA due dates (30 days Major / 60 days Minor), and the 90-day follow-up audit trigger for Major findings

Learning Objectives

By the end of this session, participants will be able to:

  • Explain the difference between internal audit, certification audit, and customer audit
  • Apply the independence rule to correctly assign (or decline) an audit assignment
  • Prepare an audit checklist and conduct an audit using document review, sampling, observation, and interview
  • Correctly classify a finding as Major Nonconformance, Minor Nonconformance, or Observation/OFI
  • Complete an Internal Audit Report (QF-004) within the 5-business-day deadline
  • Explain how audit findings flow into QP-009 CAPA and into Management Review reporting

Recommended audience:

Qualified/trained internal auditors only Quality Manager

Suggested periodicity: Full training at designation as an internal auditor, with a refresher at least annually and whenever QP-010 is revised; not part of general or new-hire training.
