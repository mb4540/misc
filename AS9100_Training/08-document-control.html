8. Document Control

How Jemba9 numbers, drafts, reviews, approves, distributes, and retires controlled QMS documents

Student Introduction

Document Control is the process that makes the rest of the QMS trustworthy. If nobody controls which version of a procedure is current, employees end up working from stale instructions, auditors can't establish traceability, and "the document said so" stops meaning anything. QP-001 defines how every controlled document at Jemba9 — from the Quality Manual down to a blank inspection form — gets a number, moves through review and approval, gets published, and eventually gets retired.

Every controlled document carries a number built from the scheme in QP-007-ANNEX-A: Level 1 manuals and policies (QM-, QP-POL-, QP-OBJ-), Level 2 procedures (QP-), Level 3 work instructions (QW-), and Level 4 forms and records (QF-). Every document file also opens with a required YAML front-matter block (QP-007-ANNEX-B) capturing its number, title, type, level, clause references, owner role, revision letter, and status. That front-matter isn't decorative — it's built to map field-for-field onto the database tables of the future SAG QMS application, so documents authored under this convention today can be ingested later with no re-keying.

Every document moves through the same lifecycle: draft → in-review → approved → obsolete. A draft isn't controlled and must never be used for real work. Once a draft is stable, the owner moves it to in-review and solicits reviewer comments; once approved, the Quality Manager publishes it to the single controlled publication point — the SharePoint QMS library — and merges it to the main branch of the Jemba9_QMS CodeCommit repository. Anything printed or downloaded from SharePoint becomes an uncontrolled "reference copy" the moment it leaves the library, which is why every controlled document is footed with "Uncontrolled when printed." Obsolete documents are watermarked, archived, and retained for a minimum of seven years.

The review step deserves special attention because it is exactly the mechanism this training portal uses. Per docs/review-notes-guide.md, reviewers submit section-by-section comments as structured Markdown files under review-notes/{docNumber}/ on the qms-review-notes branch; each note carries a status of open, actioned, or dismissed, and the document owner must action or dismiss every open note (with a written reason) before the document can advance to approved. AS9100D clause 7.5.3 requires that this review evidence be retained as part of the audit trail — which is precisely why review notes are never deleted, and why the "Reviewer Notes" fieldset at the bottom of every page in this training program, including this one, is built to mirror that same real workflow: when you print or save this page after adding your comments, you are creating exactly the kind of retained review-evidence record that clause 7.5.3 and QP-001 §3.2 require. This is not a coincidence — it's the point.

Who needs this training: Every employee who creates, reviews, approves, or relies on a controlled document — which in practice is every employee, but especially document owners, the Quality Manager, and anyone submitting or actioning review comments.

Syllabus & Training Goals

Class Syllabus

  1. Why document control matters: traceability, auditability, and "which version is current" as a compliance and mission-assurance issue
  2. The document numbering scheme: Level 1–4 prefixes (QM-, QP-POL-, QP-OBJ-, QP-, QW-, QF-) and the folder-to-level mapping
  3. Required YAML front-matter fields (docNumber, title, docType, level, clauseRef, owner, revision, status, approvedDate) and the rule against personal names or credentials in metadata
  4. The document lifecycle: draft → in-review → approved → obsolete, and who is authorized to use a document at each stage
  5. Document creation and numbering request process, and the role of the Document Register in SharePoint
  6. Review and approval workflow: SME review, front-matter status updates, owner sign-off, merge to main
  7. Controlled copy vs. reference/uncontrolled copy, and why SharePoint is the single controlled publication point
  8. The reviewer-notes mechanism: file format, folder structure, status values (open/actioned/dismissed), and the document owner's triage responsibilities
  9. Retention requirements per clause 7.5.3 (7 years minimum) and how this training portal's own reviewer-notes fieldset mirrors the real process

Learning Objectives

By the end of this session, participants will be able to:

  • Identify the document level and type of any controlled document from its number alone
  • Name the four lifecycle statuses and state which one(s) authorize a document for actual use
  • Explain why a printed or downloaded document is automatically "uncontrolled" and where to get the controlled version
  • Describe how to submit a reviewer comment and what happens to it (open → actioned/dismissed) before a document can be approved
  • State the retention requirement for approved and obsolete documents and review-note records
  • Locate the Document Register and the SharePoint QMS library

Recommended audience:

All employees (mandatory) Document owners Quality New hire onboarding Engineering

Suggested periodicity: Once at onboarding for all employees; refresher training for document owners and reviewers whenever the numbering scheme, front-matter convention, or review-notes workflow is revised, and otherwise at the annual QMS awareness refresher.
