Student Introduction

Production and inspection is where Jemba9's design and supplier-qualification work becomes a physical, deliverable product — PCB assemblies from J9CM, mechanical assemblies, cable harnesses, and integrated systems. QP-008 governs the entire path a unit takes from work order to shipment: planning, manufacturing, in-process inspection, first article inspection, final acceptance, and post-delivery support. It also governs the calibration program that keeps every inspection measurement trustworthy, and the product safety assessment that has to be completed before a new program is ever allowed to start production.

Before any production begins, the Manufacturing Manager confirms that released design documentation, a Cetec ERP work order, a traveler, lot-traced materials, calibrated equipment, and qualified personnel are all in place. For new programs or significant design changes, a Product Safety Assessment (QF-015) must be completed and approved by the Quality Manager and Chief Engineer first — identifying safety-critical characteristics, failure modes with safety consequences, and the mandatory inspection points that flow into the traveler. Production cannot start on a new program without an approved QF-015.

Every unit or lot is accompanied by a traveler that records what was done, by whom, and what was inspected, with defined inspection point types — first article, in-process, hold point, witness point, and final inspection. A First Article Inspection (FAI), using the AS9102-aligned checklist (QF-009), is required on the first unit of any new design, after form/fit/function changes, after a production lapse, or when a customer contract requires it; it verifies part accountability, material and process conformance, and every drawing characteristic before the design is approved for production. No product ships without final acceptance: all traveler operations signed off, hold points cleared, FAI complete (first article only), NCRs dispositioned, and a Certificate of Conformance issued.

Underlying all of this is the calibration program (QF-011, clause 7.1.5): every instrument used to verify conformance is NIST-traceable, tracked for calibration due dates, and immediately pulled from service if out of tolerance, with an impact assessment on any product measured with it since its last known-good calibration. When work moves between facilities, work centers, or subcontractors, the Work Transfer Checklist (QF-018) confirms documentation, personnel qualification, equipment/calibration, and quality flow-downs all transferred correctly before the first unit is built at the new location. Clause 8.5.3 (customer property) is one part of QP-008 that applies conditionally: it only comes into play when a customer supplies Customer-Furnished Equipment (CFE) or Government-Furnished Equipment (GFE) — per Paul Rutt's reviewer note on QM-001 (June 2, 2026), “Clause 8.5.3 Applicable only when customers provide customer furnished equipment (CFE) and/or government-furnished equipment (GFE). When applicable, QP-008 governs” — and when it applies, QP-008 §9 governs receipt, inspection, storage, protection, and return of that equipment.

Who needs this training: Manufacturing Manager and J9CM production operators, the Quality Manager and Quality Inspectors, the Chief Engineer, Operations Manager, and Program Managers who manage customer-furnished equipment and post-delivery support.

Syllabus & Training Goals

Class Syllabus

  1. Operational planning prerequisites before production starts: documentation, work orders, materials, calibrated equipment, qualified personnel
  2. Product Safety Assessment (QF-015): when required, what it evaluates, and why production cannot start without it — including the open reviewer note on the Section 8 approval signature block (CEO vs. COO)
  3. Work orders and travelers in Cetec ERP: required contents and sign-off blocks
  4. Identification and traceability through production: receiving labels, WIP tags, accept/hold tagging, lot traceability for safety-critical components
  5. Inspection point types (first article, in-process, hold point, witness point, final) and how they're defined on the traveler
  6. First Article Inspection (QF-009): AS9102 Form 1/2/3 methodology, FAI triggers, and approval before production ships
  7. Final acceptance and product release: closing NCRs, reviewing test data, Certificate of Conformance
  8. Calibration program (QF-011): NIST traceability, due-date tracking, and the out-of-tolerance impact-assessment protocol
  9. Customer property (clause 8.5.3, CFE/GFE) and work transfer (QF-018) between facilities or subcontractors

Learning Objectives

By the end of this session, participants will be able to:

  • List the prerequisites the Manufacturing Manager must confirm before production begins
  • Explain when a Product Safety Assessment (QF-015) is required and why production cannot proceed without it
  • Complete traveler sign-offs correctly at each defined inspection point
  • Execute an FAI using the AS9102-aligned QF-009 checklist and interpret a pass/fail result
  • Identify what to do when a calibrated instrument is found out of tolerance
  • State when clause 8.5.3 (CFE/GFE) applies and where it is governed (QP-008 §9)

Recommended audience:

J9CM Production Operators Manufacturing Manager Quality Inspectors Quality Manager Chief Engineer Program Managers

Suggested periodicity: Full session at onboarding for production, inspection, and engineering roles; annual refresher for all; additional targeted refresher whenever QF-015 or the calibration program changes (e.g., once the open CEO/COO reviewer note on QF-015 is actioned).
