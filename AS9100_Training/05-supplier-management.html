5. Supplier Management

Qualifying, approving, and monitoring the external suppliers that make Jemba9's Spacify™ model trustworthy

Student Introduction

Supplier management might sound like a back-office procurement function, but at Jemba9 it is one of the load-bearing pillars of the entire business model. Jemba9 does not build every component of a space product from raw materials up — the Spacify™ process takes proven commercial computers, controllers, data routers, power supplies, robotics, and sensors and adapts them for the space environment. That means Jemba9's finished hardware is only as trustworthy as the suppliers whose parts and services go into it. This topic covers QP-005, the procedure that governs how Jemba9 selects, qualifies, monitors, and (when necessary) removes those suppliers.

The centerpiece of the procedure is the Approved Supplier List (QF-012), maintained by the Supply Chain Manager in SharePoint. Only suppliers on the ASL may be used for procurement, and each entry carries a commodity scope, an approval status (Approved / Conditional / Suspended / Removed), and a current scorecard score. New suppliers are qualified using one of three methods scaled to risk — a questionnaire and trial PO for low-risk COTS items, up to a full on-site or third-party audit plus first article inspection for safety-critical, sole-source, or flight-heritage hardware. That risk-scaled approach is exactly what lets Jemba9 move fast on commercial-technology adaptation without cutting corners on the items that actually matter for mission success.

Every purchase order to an ASL supplier carries mandatory flow-down requirements: conformance to drawing and specification, a Certificate of Conformance with every shipment, notification of nonconformance before shipment, Jemba9's and the customer's right of access to the supplier's facilities and records, and — for any supplier receiving controlled technical data — ITAR/EAR compliance. Electronic component suppliers additionally carry counterfeit parts prevention flow-down (covered in depth in Topic 6). All active suppliers are evaluated at least annually using the Supplier Evaluation Form (QF-006), a weighted scorecard covering on-time delivery (30%), incoming quality (30%), responsiveness (20%), and documentation (20%); scores below 70 trigger a Supplier Corrective Action Request (SCAR), and scores below 50 mean suspension from the ASL.

This is why supplier management is doing double duty at Jemba9. On one level it is ordinary quality control — making sure parts meet spec and suppliers perform. On another level, it is the mechanism that makes the entire "adapt commercial technology for space" business case credible to government and prime-contractor customers: the ASL, the scorecards, and the flow-down requirements are the evidence that a COTS-sourced part reaching Jemba9's production floor is genuinely fit for orbital service. Two of Jemba9's Quality Objectives are tracked directly from this process — QO-04 Supplier On-Time Delivery ≥95% and QO-08 Incoming Rejection Rate ≤2% — and both roll up from the QF-006 scorecard data that Supply Chain Managers generate under this procedure.

Who needs this training: Supply Chain Managers, buyers/procurement staff, the Quality Manager, Receiving Inspectors, and Program Managers who identify sole-source or customer-directed suppliers.

Syllabus & Training Goals

Class Syllabus

  1. Why supplier management is core to the Spacify™ business model, not just procurement housekeeping
  2. The Approved Supplier List (QF-012): fields, approval statuses, and where it lives in SharePoint
  3. Supplier qualification methods scaled to risk — low, medium, and high — and when each applies (COTS vs. custom vs. safety-critical/sole-source)
  4. The Supplier Qualification Questionnaire and the qualification decision (Approved / Conditional / Rejected)
  5. Mandatory PO content and quality flow-down requirements, including ITAR/EAR and counterfeit parts prevention flow-down
  6. The Supplier Evaluation Form (QF-006): scorecard criteria, weighting, and scoring thresholds (Preferred / Acceptable / Conditional / Suspended)
  7. Supplier Corrective Action Requests (SCARs): when they're issued, the 10-business-day response window, and effectiveness monitoring
  8. Incoming inspection's role as the last checkpoint before nonconforming supplier material enters production
  9. Roles and responsibilities: Supply Chain Manager, Quality Manager, Program Manager, Receiving Inspector

Learning Objectives

By the end of this session, participants will be able to:

  • Explain why only ASL suppliers may be used for procurement and how a supplier gets added
  • Select the correct qualification method (questionnaire, FAI, or audit) for a given risk level
  • List the minimum PO requirements and quality flow-downs that must appear on every purchase order
  • Interpret a QF-006 scorecard result and identify the required action at each threshold
  • Describe the SCAR process and the consequences of a supplier falling below score thresholds
  • Locate the ASL, scorecards, and qualification records in SharePoint

Recommended audience:

Supply Chain / Procurement Quality Manager Receiving Inspection Program Managers J9CM Production Leads

Suggested periodicity: Full session at onboarding for supply chain and quality roles; annual refresher aligned with the ASL's annual review cycle and Management Review reporting on QO-04 and QO-08.
