6. Counterfeit Parts Prevention

Keeping counterfeit and suspect-counterfeit electronic parts out of Jemba9's space hardware

Student Introduction

Of every topic in this training program, counterfeit parts prevention sits closest to Jemba9's specific supply chain exposure. Jemba9 builds space-borne electronics, and its own J9CM PCB assembly division sources components partly from the open commercial market as part of the Spacify™ adaptation model — taking proven commercial parts and qualifying them for space use. That is precisely the supply chain profile that carries the highest counterfeit electronic parts risk in the industry: commercially available components, sourced quickly to meet program schedules, are the exact category that counterfeiters target with re-marked, recycled, or otherwise misrepresented parts. AS9100D clause 8.1.4 exists because of this risk, and QP-006 is Jemba9's answer to it.

The first line of defense is sourcing discipline: components must come from the Original Component Manufacturer (OCM), an OCM-authorized distributor, or an OCM-franchised distributor. Independent distributors — not franchised by the OCM — may only be used when an authorized source cannot supply the part, and only with prior Quality Manager approval plus enhanced controls. Certain sources are flatly prohibited without written Quality Manager approval: online auction/marketplace sites, unqualified brokers, and any source appearing on a GIDEP or ERAI counterfeit alert list.

Detection happens at receiving. Every electronic component gets a standard screening — documentation review, packaging inspection, and a visual check for re-marking or inconsistent labeling. Anything from an independent distributor, or anything that raises a flag at standard inspection, escalates to more rigorous testing: an acetone wipe test for re-marking, dimensional measurement against the OCM datasheet, X-ray inspection of the die, electrical functional test, or referral to an AS6081-accredited third-party lab. If a part is suspected counterfeit at any point — receiving, production, or a field return — the rule is simple: stop work, quarantine it in a red "SUSPECT COUNTERFEIT — DO NOT USE" container, and notify the Quality Manager within 4 hours. Confirmed counterfeit parts are never returned to stock or resold; they are physically destroyed, with a sample retained only if legal action or a GIDEP report needs evidence.

Confirmed counterfeit events also carry reporting obligations that reach outside Jemba9: government programs get reported to GIDEP, commercial programs to ERAI, and any part that may have reached a deliverable product triggers customer notification within the contractually required window (typically 24–72 hours), an NCR, and a CAPA. Every counterfeit or suspect-counterfeit event is also reviewed at Management Review as a supplier quality event and logged in the risk register. Because this risk sits at the intersection of ITAR/EAR-controlled hardware and open commercial-market sourcing, every employee touching procurement, receiving, or production is trained on it as part of QMS onboarding — not just the Quality team.

Who needs this training: Receiving Inspectors, J9CM production and procurement staff, the Quality Manager, Supply Chain Manager, and Program Managers; awareness-level training is required for all employees who may handle electronic components.

Syllabus & Training Goals

Class Syllabus

  1. Why the Spacify™ commercial-sourcing model creates elevated counterfeit parts exposure for electronic components
  2. Key definitions: counterfeit part, suspect counterfeit part, authorized source, independent distributor, GIDEP, ERAI
  3. Authorized sourcing hierarchy: OCM, OCM-authorized distributor, OCM-franchised distributor — and how to verify each
  4. Independent distributor controls: when permitted, required approvals, and prohibited sources (auction sites, unqualified brokers, GIDEP/ERAI-listed sources)
  5. Traceability requirements from OCM to Jemba9 receiving, and retention in Cetec ERP / SharePoint
  6. Standard vs. escalated receiving inspection: documentation review, packaging/label checks, acetone wipe test, dimensional check, X-ray, electrical test, third-party AS6081 lab
  7. The suspect counterfeit process: stop work, quarantine (red-bag), 4-hour notification, investigation, and disposition (destruction, evidence retention, or authentic release)
  8. Customer and contractual notification requirements when suspect/confirmed counterfeit material may be in a deliverable
  9. GIDEP and ERAI reporting obligations and internal reporting at Management Review

Learning Objectives

By the end of this session, participants will be able to:

  • Explain why Jemba9's business model carries elevated counterfeit parts risk for electronic components
  • Distinguish authorized sources from independent distributors and state the approval required to use the latter
  • Recognize the visual and documentation indicators that trigger escalated inspection
  • Execute the stop-work / quarantine / 4-hour notification sequence for a suspect counterfeit part
  • Identify when a GIDEP report, an ERAI report, or a customer notification is required
  • Locate the counterfeit event, disposition, and reporting records in SharePoint

Recommended audience:

J9CM Production & Procurement Receiving Inspection Quality Manager Supply Chain Manager Program Managers All employees (awareness level)

Suggested periodicity: Full session at onboarding for procurement, receiving, and production roles; annual refresher for those roles; a shorter awareness refresher for all other employees at annual QMS training, given how quickly counterfeit detection techniques and threat sources evolve.
