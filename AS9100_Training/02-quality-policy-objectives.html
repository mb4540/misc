2. Quality Policy & Objectives

Jemba9's Quality Policy commitments and the ten measurable Quality Objectives that operationalize them

Student Introduction

The Quality Policy is Jemba9's top-level promise about how it does business, and the Quality Objectives are how that promise gets checked every month. Together they form the shortest, most important document pair in the entire QMS — short enough that every employee should be able to recite the gist of the policy from memory, and concrete enough that every employee's work shows up somewhere in the ten objectives.

The policy commits Jemba9 to six things: Customer Value, Speed with Assurance, Continuous Improvement, Risk-Based Decisions, Product Safety, and Employee Engagement. Notice how directly this maps to the Spacify™ business model: "Speed with Assurance" is essentially a restatement of the Spacify pitch — adapt commercial technology fast, without cutting the corners that make space hardware trustworthy. "Product Safety" is non-negotiable because a nonconformance that would be a warranty claim on the ground can be a total mission loss on orbit. The policy is signed by the CEO and reviewed for continued suitability at every Management Review, which is what makes it a living commitment rather than a poster on a wall.

The policy is required to be posted in every Jemba9 facility and in the SharePoint QMS library, communicated to all employees at onboarding and at least annually at all-hands meetings, and available to interested parties (customers, auditors, the registrar) on request. That's not paperwork for its own sake — AS9100D clause 5.2 requires the organization to demonstrate that the policy is actually known and understood at every level, not just filed away.

The Quality Objectives (QO-01 through QO-10) turn each policy commitment into a number with a target, an owner, a measurement method, and a review cadence: things like On-Time Delivery (≥95%), First Pass Yield (≥98%), CAPA Closure (≥90% within 30 days), and Training Completion (100% within 30 days). Nearly every objective pulls its data from a system employees already touch daily — Cetec ERP for delivery, purchase order, and receipt records, and QMS records like the inspection log, CAPA log, and audit schedule for the rest. Because these ten metrics are reported to leadership at every Management Review, they function as Jemba9's KPI dashboard: if you can name the objective that your role feeds, you understand how your daily work is measured at the company level.

Who needs this training: Every employee, as part of onboarding and annual QMS awareness refresher training; department leads and Program Managers additionally need working fluency in the specific objectives their function owns or feeds data into.

Syllabus & Training Goals

Class Syllabus

  1. The six Quality Policy commitments and what each one means in plain language
  2. Reading the policy as a direct restatement of the Spacify™ business model (speed + assurance, not speed instead of assurance)
  3. CEO sign-off and what that signature legally and organizationally commits Jemba9 to
  4. Policy communication and availability requirements: posting, onboarding, annual all-hands, availability to interested parties on request
  5. How the policy connects to Quality Objectives — walking the Policy Commitment → Objective mapping table
  6. All ten Quality Objectives (QO-01 through QO-10): metric, target, owner, and data source for each
  7. Where each objective's data actually comes from — Cetec ERP, inspection records (QP-008), CAPA log (QP-009), audit schedule (QP-010), training records (QF-010)
  8. Review cadence and governance: monthly/quarterly/per-program review, and reporting to Management Review (QP-011)
  9. Identifying which objective(s) your own role's work feeds into

Learning Objectives

By the end of this session, participants will be able to:

  • Recite the six Quality Policy commitments and give a one-sentence example of each in a Jemba9 context
  • Name all ten Quality Objectives (QO-01–QO-10) and state the target for at least five of them
  • Identify which Quality Objective(s) their own role or department directly affects
  • Explain where objective data is captured (e.g., Cetec ERP, inspection records, CAPA log) for at least three objectives
  • State how often the Quality Policy and Objectives are formally reviewed and by whom
  • Locate the current approved Quality Policy and Quality Objectives documents in the SharePoint QMS library

Recommended audience:

All employees (mandatory) New hire onboarding Program Managers Quality Manufacturing / J9CM Supply Chain

Suggested periodicity: Once at onboarding, with a mandatory annual refresher at the all-hands QMS awareness session (consistent with the policy's own required annual communication cadence and monthly/quarterly objective review cycle).
